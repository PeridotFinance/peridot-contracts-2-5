# Cash-only pilot: liquidator executor (build step 3) — reviewable

Status: **DRAFT, for the operator's review.** Built and tested on a **local fork only**. No live run, no real key (the tests sign with THROWAWAY Foundry keystores created inside the test run), nothing signed or broadcast on any network. Code: `operator/src/executor.ts` (logic), `operator/src/txkit.ts` (fee cap, send/wait), `operator/src/signer.ts` (the external signer abstraction, shared with the treasury keeper). Spec it reproduces: `avalanche-cash-only-liquidation-cycle.md` (study) and its Foundry suite `AvalancheCashOnlyLiquidationCycleFork`.

## 1. What one executor does (one process, one host, one keystore, one USDC inventory per operator)
Each step: read the chain at one block; refuse to act on a zero (stale/unreadable) oracle price; check the fee cap and the AVAX gas floor; **(a)** redeem any pWAVAX and convert any WAVAX left from an earlier step; **(b)** take the indexed borrower with the **largest shortfall**; repay `min(close factor x debt, 0.924 x collateral value, inventory)` (a partial liquidation with all the inventory if that is less, nothing below the 1 USDC minimum); **approve the exact amount, simulate, send `liquidateBorrow`, read back** (USDC pulled == repay, pWAVAX received, LiquidateBorrow event, allowance back to 0); **redeem** the pWAVAX (read-back: WAVAX received == pTokens x exchange rate); **swap** all WAVAX to USDC on **Uniswap V3 0.05%** with **min-out = the oracle's (Chainlink) value minus the slippage bound (100 bp)**: exact approval, simulation, send, read-back, allowance back to 0. One liquidation round per step; the next round is funded from whatever USDC the operator then holds. The operator wallet has **no admin right on any contract**; `liquidateBorrow` is permissionless. Liquidators are **not** gated by the reference price (the TWAP lags a real crash by up to 30 minutes; gating liquidations on it would be dangerous).

**A min-out block stops the conversion and alerts, with no fallback route** (the tested behaviour): the swap is refused when its simulation falls below the min-out or the transaction reverts at it; the allowance is reset to 0, the WAVAX stays in the wallet (price exposure), a critical `executor:swap-blocked` alert stays active until a swap succeeds, the limit is never loosened. Liquidations continue while the inventory lasts.

Discipline (same as the keeper): a **pending marker** is persisted before each stage's first transaction and cleared when the stage finishes; one found at start-up **halts** the executor; anything unexpected after a send (an exception, a read-back mismatch) **halts** it after a best-effort revocation of both allowances; a halt needs a human to clear `executor.halted` / `executor.pending` in the state file. Losing a race to another liquidator (simulation fails, or the transaction reverts after a passing simulation) is **not** a halt: the allowance is reset and a 30 s per-borrower cooldown prevents a retry storm.

## 2. Tests: the cycle suite reproduced by the executor on a fork (block 99,200,000, real Uniswap pool, real Chainlink at the pin, mocked feeds only to move the price)
`operator/test/fork/cycle.fork.test.ts`: **31 tests, all pass**: the full matrix of 30 (`avalanche-cash-only-operator-evidence/cycle-fork-tests.txt`, about 11 minutes) and a 31st (held WAVAX is converted by a later step) added afterwards and run with the discipline group (8/8). Same mechanics as the study: borrowers take 100% of their borrowing power while the feed is mocked UP (price at borrowing = pin / (1 - gap)), the feed returns to the real price, each operator is an executor with its own keystore and inventory.
- **Case 1 (single borrower, gap 35%)**, PW30 caps 1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000 and PW40 cap 5,000, plus PW30 cap 5,000 at gap 10%: recycling clears the account in 2 rounds with no bad debt; total repaid = 0.75 x debt; funding WITH recycling = 0.50 x debt; funding WITHOUT recycling = 0.75 x debt; conversion output >= 99.8% of the oracle value; **the funding numbers are exact**: the measured peak need funds every step, **one unit less leaves a step unfunded**; the no-recycling sum funds every step and spends the inventory to 0; 90% of it leaves debt behind.
- **Case 2 (concurrent borrowers, two operators interleaved, each holding cap/2)**: PW30 cap 5,000 with 3 and 5 borrowers, PW40 cap 5,000 with 3, PW30 cap 1,000 with 5: 0 unfunded steps, all accounts cleared, same total repayments as one operator in sequence; without recycling repayments are bounded by the inventories; sequential peak need exact (one unit less leaves a step unfunded).
- **Case 3 (one operator unavailable, B alone)**: with recycling and cap/2 or cap/4 inventory every round is funded, no account stays in shortfall, no bad debt at the second fall (-60% from the pin); without recycling at cap/2 the inventory runs out (unfunded steps, accounts left in shortfall).
- **Case 5 (the min-out limit)**: an adverse pool move of 300 bp against a 100 bp limit (and 100 bp against 50 bp) **blocks the conversion**: no swap, WAVAX held, no allowance left to the router, critical alert, **not a halt**; with inventory cap/2 only the first round is funded (0.50 x debt), with inventory = cap both rounds are funded without any swap (0.75 x debt), five borrowers leave accounts in shortfall; a 30 bp move inside the limit swaps at >= 99.4% of the oracle. PW30 and PW40.
- **Discipline**: no action at a stale (1,201 s) or zero oracle price while accounts are in shortfall (nonce stays 0; it liquidates once the price is healthy); a pending marker or halted state stops everything; a wrong signer is refused; fee above the cap and AVAX below the floor send nothing; a competing liquidator is first -> simulation fails, allowance reset, cooldown, no halt; the swap reverts at the min-out between simulation and send -> blocked, allowance reset, no halt; an unexpected error while sending the swap -> allowances revoked and the executor halts.
- **Mutants**: ten executor mutants (close-factor cap removed, min-out ignored, stale-price guard removed, halt flag ignored, fee cap removed, pending marker ignored, gas floor removed, allowance not reset after a blocked swap, leftover conversion never retried, liquidation simulation result ignored): **nine killed, one equivalent** (a rejected liquidation reverts, so ignoring the simulation's error code changes nothing here); one mutant first survived and led to the 31st test. See `exec-mutants.txt`.

**Differences from the Foundry suite, stated.** Only a SUBSET of its grid is run (the table above names it); the executors run their own algorithm step by step rather than a scripted round order; **the ordering policy differs**: the executor serves the account with the largest shortfall first (rounds spread over all accounts), the study finishes one borrower before starting the next, so the stage-2 bad debt of case 3 without recycling (USD 26.6 to 133.0 in the study) is **not** reproduced and is not asserted (in the executor's order it was 0 in the scenario run; it depends on the policy); every transaction mines a block (a few blocks of interest); accounts are impersonated; the research "fallback route" variant is not an executor behaviour. Gas per round was not re-measured.

## 3. Findings of this step that matter for the live plan
- **The private RPC endpoint is a free tier that allows `eth_getLogs` over at most 10 blocks.** The borrower index (events from the deployment block) and the keeper's chain-based daily-cap check both scan logs. The process now **refuses to start** if the endpoint does not serve `monitor.logChunkBlocks` blocks, and the keeper pays nothing if it cannot verify its 24 h spend. **Upgrade the plan (pay-as-you-go) before the hosts go live**, or run with `logChunkBlocks: 10` (workable only with a persistent state file: a restart on an ephemeral filesystem would rescan from the deployment block at 10 blocks per request).
- A bug found by the keeper tests: the first draft's ABI lacked two functions (a payment would have been mined and then halted at the read-back); fixed, with a unit test that checks every encoded function against its ABI (also for the executor).
- Test harness only: ethers v5's `tx.wait()` can stall on an instant-mining local node; the fork tests use a provider with a direct receipt poll. Production code uses the stock provider against a real chain (not exercised against the real chain).

## 4. Not done / limits
- Never run on mainnet; the real chain's latency, MEV and competing liquidators are untested (the study did not test them either). The executor reads borrowers from the event index; a borrower missed by the index is not liquidated by it.
- It converts through one venue only (Uniswap V3 0.05%); depth limits of the study apply (USD 10,000 per round was the largest cycle size tested).
- A halted executor does not liquidate: the two operators are independent so that one halt does not stop the other, and the alerts say when one is down.
- Executor state on an ephemeral filesystem is lost on restart (halt flags, pending markers, cooldowns); see the host setup document.
