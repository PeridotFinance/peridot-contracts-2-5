# PharaohDlmmVault: independent adversarial review (W10), 15 Nov 2026

Reviewer: W10, read-only. Code reviewed: integration worktree `.wt-dlmm-int`, branch `feat/dlmm-vault-int`, HEAD `a6e1cb70` (includes fix `182d272c`, a future `updatedAt` is a bad feed; `contracts/vault-dlmm/src` unchanged between my read and my final check). Files: `contracts/vault-dlmm/src/PharaohDlmmVault.sol` (840 lines), `PharaohDlmmPairValidator.sol`, `IPharaohDlmmVault.sol`, `interfaces/`, `DEVIATIONS.md`, spec, evaluation sections 7 and 8, risk register, ceremony, and the R3 base for comparison. I did not rely on the tests (I read three of them where cited). Nothing was signed, sent or pushed; no chain access was used. Labels: MEASURED (I ran or read it), DERIVED (arithmetic on stated figures or on the code), ESTIMATED (assumption). Line numbers are in `PharaohDlmmVault.sol` unless named.

## Verdict
No Critical or High finding in the contract logic. One Medium finding blocks the ceremony as currently written (F1, first-depositor and dust-supply inflation: real theft from a depositor, fix is procedural plus an optional guard). Two further Mediums concern the keeper bound (F2, F3) and have small code fixes; I recommend F2 before the ceremony (it changes the codehash, so tests, W7 and the fork run must be repeated). The operator must knowingly accept F3 and the hook dependency (risk register row 1).

## Findings, ranked

### F1. Medium, BLOCKS the ceremony as written. ERC-4626 first-depositor / dust-supply inflation is profitable for the attacker at supply greater than 1 share-wei (lines 195, 214-221, 305-309; DEVIATIONS 5.5; `test/Shares.t.sol:154` and `test/Invariant.t.sol:67`)
This is "the dust-supply finding". Entry pricing uses the OZ virtual offset 0 (virtual 1 share, 1 asset): shares = d x (S+1)/(N+1). Redemption, however, is pro rata on the REAL supply (lines 305-309). The two disagree at small S, and the donation counts in NAV (idle balance). The existing test `test_firstDepositorInflationAttackIsBoundedByTheCap` only tries an attacker with 1 share-wei; with 1 share the attack does not pay (I verified: the victim never loses beyond rounding), which is why that test passes. With 4 shares it does:
- Attacker deposits 4 wei (4 shares), then transfers USDC straight to the vault until NAV = 142,857,142.
- Victim deposits d = 57,142,856: shares = floor(57,142,856 x 5 / 142,857,143) = 1. The cap check passes (142,857,142 + 57,142,856 = 199,999,998).
- Real supply is 5. Victim redeems 1/5 of 199,999,998 = 39,999,999 (a loss of USD 17.14, 30 % of the deposit); the attacker redeems 4/5 = 159,999,998 for a cost of 142,857,142, profit USD 17.14. DERIVED by integer arithmetic.
General: with attacker shares k and cap T = 200e6, loss = T(k-1)/((k+1)(k+3)), maximal at k = 4 (USD 17.1); a victim deposit above USD 80 (the bound is 2 x cap/(k+3) with k >= 2) is not exploitable, a deposit of exactly the cap is safe, anything smaller is exposed. Exposure windows: (a) between `unpause` and the Ledger's first deposit if they are separate transactions (the vault starts paused, line 181, and `deposit` is `whenNotPaused`, so seeding is impossible before `unpause`; the attacker tunes k and the donation to the deposit size announced in the Safe queue or visible in the mempool, and recovers his donation through his 4/5 share); (b) any later state where supply is dust (everyone but a few wei has exited) and a depositor of less than about USD 80 enters. Cost to the attacker is gas plus temporary capital of about USD 143.
Fix, in order of preference: (1) ceremony: ONE Safe MultiSend [`unpause`, `USDC.approve`, `deposit(AMOUNT, receiver)`] with `AMOUNT` of at least USD 1 made to a dead receiver (or never redeemed), so supply is never below about 1e6 share-wei; a first deposit of exactly 200 USDC is also safe but leaves no room. (2) code (about 60 B, margin is 989 B): in `deposit`, `mint`, `redeem`, `redeemInKind`, `withdraw` revert if the resulting `totalSupply()` is in (0, 1e6) (a minimum-liquidity rule), or override `_decimalsOffset()` to 6 (OZ's recommendation; shares then have 12 decimals, which changes every dust threshold, test and frontend figure, so I do not recommend it this late). (3) Update DEVIATIONS 5.5, which understates this as "a rounding loss".
Also: donations alone (attacker with 1 share) can deny entry (`ZeroShares`, `CapExceeded`) at the cost of gas, because the sole holder can `redeemInKind` the donation back.

### F2. Medium. The 0.5 % per-rebalance bound is only an immediate, oracle-marked bound; the band is minted at an unchecked post-swap price (lines 427-438, 380-396, 550-554)
`rebalance` and `openBand` check pool-versus-oracle deviation (bit 16, 60 bp) once, at entry (`_requireEntryOk`, lines 416 and 376). The vault then swaps (up to 60 % of NAV) and `_addBand` mints around the real active id read after the swap. The only checks after the swap are `_checkActive` against the CALLER's `expectedActiveId` (useless against a thief, who passes the id he engineered) and `_checkNavLoss` (line 545), which marks NAV at the ORACLE immediately after the mint. A band minted at an active price off the oracle shows no NAV loss at that instant; the loss arrives when an arbitrageur trades against the mispriced bins afterwards.
Exploit (DERIVED, not run). Variant A, pre-positioned, ordinary depth: a stolen keeper key plus anyone willing to push the pool 55 bp (inside the 60 bp gate) calls `rebalance` with `swapMinOut = 0`, `amountXMin/YMin = 0`, `expectedActiveId` = the pushed id. Swap loss up to the floor: 0.5 % of about USD 100 = USD 0.50; then the band is minted 0.55 % off the oracle and the back-run takes about 0.55 % of the about USD 100 on the mispriced side, USD 0.55. Total about USD 1.05 = 0.5 % of a 200 NAV, of which only the swap half is visible to `NavLossTooLarge`. Variant B, thin pool: a whale withdraws near-peg depth so the vault's own swap moves the active id several bins while the average fill still passes the 0.5 % floor; the post-mint deviation is then unbounded by the vault.
Honest keeper: protected, because it sets `expectedActiveId` to the predicted post-swap id with `idSlippage` <= 2.
Fix (about 60 to 150 B): after `_guardedSwap` and before `_addBand`, in both `rebalance` and `openBand`, re-check `_poolDeviates(pa, pp)` and revert `GuardUnsafe(POOL_ORACLE_DEVIATION)`. It closes variant B only; it does NOT close variant A (55 bp passes a 60 bp gate). Residual with the fix: post-mint leak <= `maxPoolOracleDevBps` (0.60 %) x the exposed side (<= about 0.3 to 0.6 % of NAV) plus the swap floor (<= 0.3 % of NAV at the 60 % swap cap): ESTIMATED up to about 0.9 % of NAV per rebalance in the worst pre-positioned case. A tighter post-swap gate (for example 35 bp, above the measured worst standing pool offset of 29 to 30 bp) would shrink variant A; I would pair it with F3.
Blocks ceremony: no (loss bounded by the 200 cap); recommended before.

### F3. Medium. The daily cap resets at UTC midnight; the worst case is 96 rebalances in about 3.2 h, not 48 a day (lines 417-424, 761-766)
Cooldown 120 s plus `_day = timestamp / 1 days` lets a keeper-key thief do 48 before and 48 after midnight: 95 x 120 s = 11,400 s = 3.17 h. DERIVED at the 200 cap, compounding, 0.5 % per rebalance as the vault bounds it:
- 48 in a day: 1 - 0.995^48 = 21.4 % of NAV = USD 42.8 (simple sum 24 % = USD 48, risk register row 6).
- 96 across midnight in 3.2 h: 1 - 0.995^96 = 38.2 % = USD 76.4.
- At the ESTIMATED F2 residual of 0.9 % per rebalance: 35 % (USD 70) for 48, 58 % (USD 116) for 96.
Each rebalance also needs the active bin outside the band, which the thief or a whale must force each time (a swap across 2 bins, cheap for a whale; two addresses hold 97 % of the pool). A stranger sandwiching an HONEST keeper's swap extracts only the slack between the keeper's `swapMinOut` and the fill; it cannot beat the vault floor (oracle minus `maxRebalanceLossBps`). Value cannot leave except as trading loss: there is no recipient argument. At the USD 200 cap a profit-seeking stranger does not gain more than about USD 1 per rebalance for the cost of moving the pool 55 bp, so this matters for a key thief aligned with a whale, or if the cap is ever raised.
Fix: a rolling loss budget instead of a day counter (a `uint32` bps budget refilled linearly, for example 50 bp per 30 min, capped at 100 bp; charge `navBefore - navAfter` per rebalance; about 150 B). Cheaper: operations (alert, Safe `pause()` within minutes; `pause` blocks `rebalance`, never exits).
Blocks ceremony: no, but the operator must accept the number above, which is larger than the "48 a day" wording suggests.

### F4. Low. `tripGuard` latch needs a Safe transaction to clear; a real Chainlink blip latches it for everyone (lines 589-602)
(a) Can anyone latch with a flash-moved feed? No: the feeds are Chainlink aggregator proxies, not movable in a transaction; the pool price is only bit 16, which is non-latchable (`LATCHABLE_MASK = 15`), so pool manipulation cannot latch (fork test `test_poolMoveAloneCannotLatch` agrees). (b) Can the latch be bypassed? No: `GuardLatched` is checked in `deposit`, `mint` (through `_requireEntryOk`), `openBand`, `rebalance`, and `maxDeposit/maxMint` return 0; `_deposit` is internal and there is no permit-deposit. (c) Cost: the calibration says a 50 bp leg band would have latched on two real USDT feed episodes since 2023 (+56 bp for 79 s, +66 bp for 17 min). Anyone can then latch during such a window; only `clearGuard` (Safe, Ledger 1-of-1) releases it, so the band stays out of range and the keeper halts until a human acts. Exits are unaffected. `clearGuard` can revert at a flickering bit 16 (DEVIATIONS 5.6).
Fix: none required; consider `legBandBps` 75 (the W-CAL option). Blocks ceremony: no.

### F5. Low. PHAR dust-burn forfeiture (risk register row 9, left to this review)
Mechanism (code: lines 264-281, 305-351): `redeemInKind` by anyone with shares calls the pair's `burn` on the vault's bins, which resets the vault's `lastModified` at the rewarder; a second touch within 111 s forfeits the PHAR accrued since the previous settlement. The attacker needs at least about 3 share-wei (below that every bin's proceeds floor to zero, the bin is skipped and the call reverts `ExitFailed`; W6 measured 3), so capital is about USD 0.000003 and the shares come back in kind.
Cost to the attacker (ESTIMATED: 0.3 to 0.4 M gas per call including the hook, 3.5 gwei median, AVAX USD 8.58): about USD 0.01 a call; one call per 111 s = 778 a day = about USD 8 a day.
Loss to the vault (DERIVED from the W6 measurement, 300 s of accrual = 5.318e15 wei PHAR for a USD 100 vault): 1.53 PHAR a day = about USD 0.063 a day at USD 0.041, about USD 0.13 a day at the 200 cap. The attacker pays roughly 60x to 130x what he destroys, and would need a PHAR emission about 60x today's to break even. The loss is the Safe's PHAR stream, not depositor principal. No code fix keeps exits ungated (a pro-rata burn must touch the bins); accept and monitor the `PharHarvested` rate. Blocks ceremony: no.

### F6. Low. `redeem`/`withdraw` have no min-out and trust the router's return value (lines 236-259, 283-300, 467-483)
(a) The USDt part of an exit is swapped with min out = oracle x (1 - 0.5 %) and the redeemer cannot tighten it: a sandwicher extracts up to 0.5 % of that part (about 0.25 % of the redemption at 50/50, USD 0.50 at 200), borne by the redeemer only. The interface is frozen; the frontend should prefer `redeemInKind` or say so. (b) `_swap` returns the router's `amountOut` and `_payOutAsset` pays `assetAmt + amountOut` (lines 295-298) without a balance delta; a router that over-reports would pay the redeemer out of other holders' idle USDC. The router is checked against the factory and the voter authorisation at init but not code-pinned. Fix (about 40 B): measure the asset balance before and after the swap. Blocks ceremony: no.

### F7. Low. Ownership: single-step transfer and `renounceOwnership` exposed (lines 28-35 inherit `OwnableUpgradeable`, line 128 `__Ownable_init`)
A mistaken transfer or renounce (both are Safe transactions) permanently loses `pause`, `unpause`, `clearGuard`, params, cap and rebalancer rotation; the vault could stay latched or paused forever (exits still work). Fix: override `renounceOwnership` to revert (about 30 B) and use a two-step flow in the ceremony. Blocks ceremony: no.

### F8. Info (batch)
- `harvestRewards` emits `PharHarvested.amount` = the vault's WHOLE PHAR balance (line 573), not the claim delta. Anyone can send PHAR to the vault and inflate the "realised PHAR rate" the spec (section 7) puts on the frontend. Fix: emit `balanceAfter - balanceBefore` of the claim (about 30 B), or tell the frontend to filter by `claim` transfers.
- Keeper griefing: any third party that moves the active id or the pool price between the keeper's read and its transaction (a swap of a few dollars across the 3 bins, or past the 60 bp gate) makes `rebalance` revert `ActiveIdMoved` or `GuardUnsafe`; the keeper never retries, so the band stays out of range until the next cycle (lost fees and PHAR, no principal loss). Cost to the griefer: gas plus swap fees of the amount moved. Low.
- Idle balances and fold-in (lines 385-391, 493-496, 516-538): idle USDC counts in NAV at par, is folded in at the next `openBand`/`rebalance`, and the rebalance's loss is shared pro rata, including by a depositor who entered just before it (entry is free, the exit is a pro-rata slice including the idle part). I found no extraction path; a depositor can try to enter just before a visible large swap to capture the vault's fee share, but the gain is bounded by the cap times the vault's share of one swap's fee (cents).
- NAV swing from a swap inside the band (my own bound, replacing the evaluation's 0.07 %): the band is 3 bins x 1 bp, but a swap moves composition at the POOL price while NAV marks at the ORACLE, so the swing is (|pool - oracle| + about 3 bp) x the shifted amount (<= half of NAV). With entries gated at 60 bp that is at most about 0.3 % of NAV (ESTIMATED, worst); in normal conditions (pool 11 bp under the oracle) about 0.07 to 0.14 %. A front-running depositor gains at most that fraction of his deposit, which at the cap is about USD 0.6 and is paid by his own sandwiched swap.
- The validator (lines 31-75) pins decimals only for the feeds; a swapped or wrong feed address (for example USDT/USD as `feedAsset`) passes if it has 8 decimals and is fresh. Read `description()` and the answers in the ceremony read-back. Initialize also accepts a feed already outside the leg band (13b checks stale/bad only); revert at init when bits 4 or 8 hold.
- `rebalanceCooldown` hard minimum 111 (line 763) equals the forfeiture window, but the rewarder forfeits at `<= 111` s; the lower bound should be 112.
- Read-only reentrancy: `redeem*` burn shares (lines 275, 289) before the bins are drained, so `totalAssets`/`convertToAssets` read mid-exit is inflated; the only external calls in that window are the pair (hook is Pharaoh code) and the two ERC-20s, so no attacker callback exists today. A consumer that prices shares (Peridot oracle) must treat `convertToAssets` as spot and handle the `FeedInvalid` revert.
- `withdraw(assets)` can pay less than `assets` and `previewRedeem` overstates (DEVIATIONS D1); ERC-4626 integrators beware.
- After a full exit (`shares == supply`, lines 306, 350) bins whose proceeds floor to zero are skipped and `_hasBand` is cleared: dust LB shares (under 1 raw unit per token per bin) stay orphaned and uncounted, re-counted only if a later band reuses the id. No value risk.
- Upgrade: the ProxyAdmin owner (Safe, 1-of-1) can replace the implementation, including the 200 ceiling, at any time with no delay (risk register row 5).
- The reward token is fixed at init (line 152), validated non-zero and not a leg (check 12), so a changed rewarder token can never make `harvestRewards` forward USDC or USDt; a hostile token can only make `harvestRewards` revert, which does not touch exits.
- Risk register row 8 and ceremony question B read as if the vault were created unpaused; the code pauses it at the end of `initialize` (line 181), so that window is closed, but see F1 for what the unpause-then-deposit gap allows.

## Areas where I found nothing
- Pause: `redeem`, `withdraw`, `redeemInKind`, `harvestRewards`, `tripGuard` carry no `whenNotPaused` (lines 236, 246, 264); only deposit, mint, openBand, rebalance do. Pause cannot block exits.
- `redeemInKind` and `_sliceOut`: no oracle read, no swap, no router call, no guard, latch, cooldown, keeper or codehash reference (lines 264-281, 305-359). Under any depeg or dead feed it works; `redeem`/`withdraw` revert `FeedInvalid` or at the swap floor, as documented.
- Reentrancy: every state-changing entry point except `tripGuard`, `clearGuard` and the admin setters is `nonReentrant`; LB shares have no ERC-1155 receiver callback (non-transferable), the tokens are plain ERC-20s.
- Approvals: `approveForAll` is never used (direct `pair.burn`); every router approval is set to 0 after use (lines 478-482, 516-538); `forceApprove` handles USDT.
- Cap: `deposit`/`mint` check `totalAssets() + assets <= cap` (line 727); a donation only reduces headroom; gains can exceed the cap (D6); no direct-transfer, mint or rebasing path bypasses it.
- Rounding: deposit floors, mint and withdraw ceil, in-kind floors, skipped dust bins stay with holders; apart from the offset mismatch of F1 I found no farmable rounding.
- Gas DoS: the band is fixed at 3 bins; no per-user loops. Overflow: plain `a*b/c` bounded far below 2^256 (D5), reasoned against a worst case of 1e16 raw units x 2^128 shares per unit.
- Proxy and init: the implementation constructor calls `_disableInitializers`; the validator is stateless with no selfdestruct or delegatecall; the vault has no `delegatecall` or `selfdestruct`; storage is non-namespaced state after namespaced OZ bases with a 40-slot gap; `initialize` sets owner and rebalancer from params, caps at the ceiling, ends paused. Initializer front-run is impossible if the proxy is created with init data in its constructor (ceremony design).
- EIP-170: MEASURED today with `forge build --sizes` (output to a scratch directory): runtime 23,587 B, margin 989 B; initcode 27,632 B; validator 3,680 B. The F1 guard, F2 and F6 fixes fit individually; with F3 and F7 together they need a size pass.
