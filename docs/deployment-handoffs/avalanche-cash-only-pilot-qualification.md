**How to read it.** (a) *Supplier money* (supply and its 20% cash floor) is not our capital. (b) *Protection capital* is the only item that can be CONSUMED by a loss; it is sized by the tail gap you choose, and it is small in absolute terms at research caps (USD 84 to 4,312). (c) *Liquidator inventory* is capital at work, not spent: it is recovered when the seized WAVAX is converted back (section 2), and is the largest line (at the 5,000 cap: about USD 5,050 across two operators for first rounds, up to about 4,450 on a single operator covering the worst tail without recycling). (d) Operating cost is cents per liquidation. **PW30 versus PW40:** PW30 survives every observed window with zero bad debt and even the year's peak-to-trough as one step with only about 1% loss (zero-loss gap 67.3% with 30 days of interest), so its protection capital is the smaller of the two at every tail, but borrowers can borrow only 30% of collateral value; PW40 survives the worst observed 30-day move (-42.9%) with 13.5 points to spare and needs about 1.8 times PW30's protection capital at -75% (and far more at -67.6%, where PW30 needs about 1% of cap and PW40 about 26%). Protection capital does not buy proportional safety: it covers a bounded tail (table below).

**Smaller pilot.** Going from cap 10,000 to 1,000 divides every line by ten; it does not change the shape. The one real saving of the one-direction pilot over a two-direction pilot is structural: one liquidator asset (USDC), no second tail, no depeg exposure in a second direction, no exit-cash problem on the collateral market. Fixed costs (monitoring, a second operator, an independent reference price, treasury operations) do not shrink with the cap, so a USD 1,000 cap is dominated by operations, not by capital.

## 2. The complete liquidation cycle and conversion routes
Detail, addresses and per-row numbers: `avalanche-cash-only-liquidation-cycle.md` (FINAL). Routes were verified on chain at the common pinned state (block 99,200,000, Chainlink mid 8.549) and real swaps executed (no mocks, amountOutMinimum measured then enforced).
- **Uniswap V3 WAVAX/USDC 0.05%:** slippage vs Chainlink -1 / 2 / 7 bp at USD 1k / 5k / 10k (negative = paid more than Chainlink), 92 bp at 100k; 1% slippage depth about USD 109k, 3% depth about USD 323k. **Best route at pilot sizes.**
- **Pharaoh (ts 10):** -2 / -1 / 0 bp at 1k / 5k / 10k but a wall near USD 98k (428 bp at 100k). Fine for small sizes, not for large.
- LFJ LB V2.2 bin 10: 8 / 34 / 67 bp (1% depth about USD 14k). LFJ V1: 55 / 247 / 477 bp. Pangolin: 222 / 964 / 1,747 bp. Other tiers/venues and Sushi: dust, fail every limit. Best split at USD 50k / 100k: Uniswap 30 to 40% + Pharaoh the rest (about 11 / 28 bp). Aggregators: not testable on a pinned fork, untested.
- **Cycle (FINAL; 8 cycle tests + 38 route tests, verified):** funded repayment -> seizure of pWAVAX -> redemption of WAVAX (the pWAVAX market has no borrowers: fully redeemable) -> conversion to USDC through Uniswap V3 0.05% with min-out = Chainlink minus 1% (output 99.91 to 99.97% of oracle value) -> next liquidation funded from the converted USDC. Net to the liquidator **+489 to +494 bp of the repaid amount per round** after gas at incentive 1.08. PW30 and PW40 behave identically in these cycles.
- **Concurrent borrowers (3 and 5, two operators interleaved):** all funded, zero bad debt; with recycling the peak need per operator falls with the number of borrowers (500 at cap 5,000 with 5 borrowers); without recycling it is 2,000 / 1,750 (cap 5,000, 5 borrowers), within cap/2 each.
- **One operator unavailable:** operator B alone is fully covered **only** with recycling (even with inventory cap/4) or with the no-recycling requirement; with cap/2 and no recycling, 4 of 6 (3 borrowers) or 7 of 10 (5 borrowers) rounds are funded, 1 to 2 accounts remain in shortfall, and a later fall to -60% turns them into bad debt (USD 26.6 to 133.0 at caps 1,000 to 5,000).
- **Route blocked by the limit (adverse move 300 bp, limit 100 bp):** with no fallback route the swap is blocked and the liquidator holds the WAVAX; inventory must then cover the no-recycling requirement (0.75 x debt in a 35% gap); with a second route all rounds proceed.
- **Funding, with and without recycling (35% gap):** with recycling 0.50 x debt per operator (500 / 2,500 / 5,000 for caps 1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000); no recycling 0.75 x debt (750 / 3,750 / 7,500), up to about 0.88 x debt in a 30-day-outage tail (890 / 4,440 / 8,890). The capital tables in section 1 show both. **Recommendation:** size each operator to the no-recycling number so that one operator can cover the pilot alone and a blocked route does not stop liquidations.

## 3. Oracle limits, outages, interest, USDC deviations (limits unchanged; secondary oracle deferred)
**Narrower observed result (replaces "no oracle availability blocker").** In the samples taken on 2026-11-03: AVAX/USD, 1,500 consecutive rounds over 38.9 h, max gap 147 s (limit 1,200 s); USDC/USD, 92 valid rounds over 90.8 days (one aggregator phase), worst lateness over the 24 h heartbeat 89 s (slack 3,511 s). **No round exceeded its limit in these samples.** This is an observation over short windows, not a bound on future feed behaviour; earlier aggregator phases and longer periods were not examined.

**What an extended outage does (fork tests `AvalancheCashOnlyPilotOutageFork.t.sol`, 4 tests; production guards unchanged).** While a feed is stale: new borrows are refused (PRICE_ERROR), liquidation is refused (PRICE_ERROR), repay works. A stale USDC feed with a fresh AVAX feed also blocks liquidation (both prices are needed). Pilot-scale markets (supply = cap/0.8), borrowers at 100% of capacity:

| Outage, then the worst observed AVAX fall for that length | interest accrued | liquidation rounds needed after resume (cap 1,000-10,000, PW30 / PW40) | repaid / debt | bad debt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 h, -6.7% | 0.0013% | 1 / 1 | 50% | 0 |
| 24 h, -12.4% | 0.033% | 1 / 1 | 50% | 0 |
| 7 d, -25.5% | 0.23% | 1 / 1 | 50% | 0 |
| 30 d, -42.9% | 0.99% | 2 / 3 | 75% / 87-88% | 0 |
**Time to liquidate after resume:** each round is one transaction in one block; 1 to 3 rounds clear these cases, so on chain it takes seconds once a fresh round is served and an operator is online; the binding time is operator detection and response (proposal: 1 hour, section 4), during which the outage price move is already realised. Rounds and percentages are independent of the cap.
**Beyond the zero-loss gap, with outage interest included** (tail -75%, cap 5,000): measured unrecoverable debt PW30 22.8% / 23.0% / 23.6% of the debt after 24 h / 7 d / 30 d of outage; PW40 42.1% / 42.2% / 42.7%. It matches the closed form with the accrued interest within 0.6 pp.
**USDC price deviations (fresh feeds):** USDC +2% and +5% with the 24 h worst fall: zero bad debt for both candidates; USDC -2% reduces the debt value (benign). A combination (USDC +10%, a 30-day outage, AVAX -42.9%) still gives zero bad debt for PW40 because the zero-loss gap only shrinks to about 52%. Rule: the zero-loss fall is `1 - CF x (1+i) x pU x LI` (pU = USDC price relative to 1.0).
**Residual risk, stated without treating history as a bound.** One year of data is one regime: its worst moves (-42.9% in 30 days, -67.6% peak-to-trough) are lower bounds of what can happen, not limits. Beyond the zero-loss gap the loss grows roughly linearly with the gap (table in section 1), and protection capital covers a bounded tail only. Outages longer than any observed would let a move accumulate without liquidation; the zero-loss gap then fails only if the move exceeds 56% (PW40) or 67% (PW30) in one go, and interest-only drift at 12% APR consumes about 0.4 point of the gap per month (PW40: 56.8% -> 56.4% after 30 days). What is NOT covered: an outage coinciding with a move beyond those gaps, a USDC deviation larger than the tested ones, a manipulated or mistaken price (the treasury detection rule requires an independent reference price for this reason), and feed behaviour outside the samples.

## 4. Treasury `repayBorrowBehalf`: preferred mechanism to qualify (no funds committed)
Full specification, tables and 14 fork tests: `avalanche-cash-only-treasury-repayment.md`. The audited write-off (S3) is a separate scaling proposal (`avalanche-cash-only-loss-handling.md`).
- **Default-detection rule** (on-chain computable except the reference price): fire only if all needed oracle and independent reference prices are non-zero, `getAccountLiquidity` returns no error, debt > 0, the account is in shortfall, `collateralUsd x 1e18 < debtUsd x LI` (even a full liquidation cannot clear it) and `collateralUsd <= USD 0.01` (liquidation has exhausted the collateral). Amount: the debt at the repay transaction, via `repayBorrowBehalf(account, type(uint256).max)`. Seven verdicts; only `DEFAULT_EXHAUSTED` pays. Truth-table tested (silent for healthy, recoverable, tail-before-liquidation, stale price, zero price). A single-source rule is fooled by a manipulated price, so an independent reference price is mandatory and its absence fails closed.
- **Authorized operator:** `repayBorrowBehalf` is permissionless, so authorization is about who holds the treasury USDC. Roles: monitor (no funds), treasury multisig (USDC and gas only, no admin power), separate liquidator operators, separate admin/guardian. Tested: the treasury key cannot pause, change factors, reduce reserves, move the floor or change the oracle; repaying a healthy account is a gift, not a theft.
- **Available funding:** per cap and tail, the table in section 1 (protection capital). Nothing is committed.
- **Response time:** waiting costs little interest (at most 0.52% of the loss after 30 days) but the exit window is first-come-first-served for as long as the debt is unrepaid: between 65.8% and 93.4% of claims can exit whole while nothing is repaid (PW40 -75%: 65.8%; -60%: 93.4%). Proposal (not derived from tests): repay within 1 hour, escalate at 4 hours, outer bound 24 hours.
- **Insufficient funding:** total exit shortfall = max(0, remaining borrows - reserves) in every one of the 24 withdrawal orders; each USDC repaid removes exactly one USDC of shortfall; the last suppliers in the queue absorb the loss. Worst supplier recovery: PW40 -75% unfunded 0% (34.4% of supply unrecoverable), 50% funded 0%; PW40 -60% unfunded 34.1%, 25% funded 50.8%, 50% funded 67.5%; PW30 -75% unfunded 0%, 50% funded 6.15%; PW30 -80% 0% / 0%. Late funding still heals suppliers who were short. Mitigations (guardian pause of borrowing and minting, raising the cash floor, partial repayment first) do not create cash; there is no redeem pause.

## 5. Parameter recommendations (all PROVISIONAL until tested together with the selected route and operators)
| Parameter | Provisional value | Why, and the trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Liquidation incentive | **1.08** (keep provisional) | Net to the liquidator after the 2.8% seize share is 497.6 bp; measured net per cycle round +489 to +494 bp after gas and the Uniswap conversion at 1k-5k sizes. 1.05 gives 206 bp (about 200 bp left after conversion costs: viable but thin at a USD 1,000 cap, where a first round repays about USD 500, i.e. about USD 10); 1.10 gives 692 bp. A higher incentive pays more to the liquidator out of collateral that would otherwise cover debt; it moves the zero-loss gap by under one point (PW40: 57.6% at 1.05, 56.4% at 1.08, 55.6% at 1.10, with 30 days of interest). It turns negative below 1.0288. |
| Close factor | **50%** (keep provisional) | It changes only the number of rounds (1 to 3 here) and the first-round inventory (0.5 x debt per operator); 25% needs more rounds and more time after an outage, 90% needs about twice the inventory. |
| Rate-model shape | keep the fixture's shape (2% base, 10% slope, jump 100% above 80%) | At the pilot's 80% utilisation (cap/supply) borrow APR is about 12.0%; the 20% cash floor keeps utilisation at or below 80% so the jump region is not reached by design. Suppliers earn about borrow APR x utilisation x (1 - reserve factor) = about 8.6% at 80% utilisation (lower at lower utilisation, not measured here). A steeper jump raises cost into stress and reserve accrual but does not help the pilot below 80%. |
| Reserve factor | **10%** | Reserve accrual is about 1.19% of borrows per year at 80% utilisation (about USD 12 per year per USD 1,000 of cap): **replenishment only**, never backing; 20% would double it and cost suppliers about 1.2 points of yield. |
| Cash floor | 20% of supply | Keeps 20% of supplier cash un-lent so exits and recoveries have cash; guards new borrows only. |

**PERIDOT token: why an address is required, and whether a no-incentive pilot needs a token.** The token is touched by exactly two entry points: `claimPeridot` and the admin `_grantPeridot` (`grantPeridotInternal` calls `balanceOf` then `transfer` on it). No lending path touches it: tested (`AvalancheCashOnlyRewardTokenIndependenceFork.t.sol`) by swapping the live controller to variants whose "token" **reverts on any call** and to a variant with **no token at all** (`getPeridotAddress() = address(0)`), then running supply, enter markets, borrow, interest accrual, repay, claim transfer, liquidation, seize, redeem and exit: all pass. The reasons an address is demanded today are mine, not the protocol's: the base `Peridottroller.getPeridotAddress()` hardcodes `0x507f...ecBE`, which has **no code on Avalanche** (tested), so inheriting it would point reward claims at nothing; the Avalanche wrapper's constructor therefore forces an explicit choice (`code.length > 0`) so nobody ships the wrong chain's address by accident. **A no-incentive pilot (all reward speeds zero) can run without introducing a new token dependency**; what it needs is a wrapper change to allow "no reward token" (accept `address(0)`; `claimPeridot` and `_grantPeridot` then revert, which is harmless when nothing accrues) with its own tests, or, if you prefer to keep the guard, an explicit decision on which contract to point at. I have not chosen a placeholder and have not changed the wrapper; this is a decision for you.
