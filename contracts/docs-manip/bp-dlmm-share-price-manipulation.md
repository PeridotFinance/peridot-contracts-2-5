1. **The stored leg cannot be poisoned into a borrowing profit.** Every accepted poke stores a value that is at most the vault's own live answer at that moment (fuzz invariant, 256 x 12 steps, T B8) and, inside the 24 h window, at most +2 % and at most once an hour (T A3, B2, B3). N pokes in one transaction store once (T B2). The only way to push the vault answer up is a donation (backed, redeemable in kind: T B3 last assert, earlier doc a1) or an exogenous feed glitch. Attacker-controlled swaps move the leg by +0.46 bp and, in both directions and with the USDT feed at par and +-10 bp off the pool, never locked in a stored value more than a few bp below the unpushed leg (T B5, C3: basis points, not a proof). The stored value is **not read at all** while the vault answers (T B1).
2. **Maximum stored value.** After n hourly pokes during a sustained glitch the stored leg is S0 x 1.02^n: x1.61 after 24 pokes, x2.04 after 36 (2x at hour 36), and the CF-50 % break-even of the 97 % fallback (0.97 x stored >= 2 x true) needs 37 consecutive hourly pokes at a glitch that lasts 37 hours (T B3, B4). A stuck USDT/USD = 3.00 doubles the live price at once (T B4: 19,999 bp) but the vault reverts 93,601 s after the feed's last update, so at most 26 hourly pokes happen (27 if the 1 h gate is already open when the glitch lands: T B4b): stored x1.673 (x1.707), fallback price x1.623 of true, borrow power 0.81 of the true collateral value: no bad debt. The unlimited LIVE price during those 26 hours is the pre-existing exposure of every version, not something the poke adds.
3. **Downward / ratchet trap.** A one-block glitch can be stored at once by anyone; the stored leg then climbs back at 2 %/h (-25 %: 15 h, -50 %: 36 h; T C1). The trap matters only if the vault is unavailable while the stored leg is still low. For a USDT feed that goes stale this needs a dip deeper than 1 - 1/1.02^26 = 40.2 % (41.4 % with the 27th poke) (T C2); even then the loss is a transfer from borrowers to a liquidator (33 % bonus at true prices in the test, 8 % is the normal incentive), lenders lose nothing. Whoever chooses WHEN to poke gains nothing: downward is whatever the vault says (swap pushes cannot lower it: T C3), upward is backed value.
4. **Fallback overvaluation window (key question).** True value / (stored x 0.97) = (1 - w x delta) / 0.97 where w = USDt fraction of the NAV and delta = real USDt discount (T D1, with the real vault and a real arbitraged pool; after a depeg the arbitrage leaves the vault holding USDt only, w = 99.99 %). **The 3 % haircut stops covering at w x delta = 3 %** (USDT depeg 3 % at w = 1, 6 % at w = 0.5). **Lender bad debt starts only at w x delta = 46 % (debt taken before the outage) / 47.6 % (debt taken in the outage)** and, at the vault's current size (NAV about 200 USDC at the deposit cap, 199 non-dead shares), is at most **96.5 USDC** (0.485 x 199; 99.5 for pre-outage debt) when USDt goes to zero with the whole NAV in the band. That figure is CONDITIONAL on the posted value staying near 200 USDC: the deposit cap gates entries only, so a donation (real value) or a higher stored leg raises the posted collateral value without a deposit (earlier doc a4; stored x1.67 in B4). **The unconditional ceiling is the pUSDC borrow cap: 200 USDC (strict <, 199.999999)**, reached at 412 USDC of posted stored value; the cash floor leaves 224. The collateral factor, not the haircut, keeps lenders whole.
5. **RESOLVED BY v3: the vault can revert for reasons that are not a stale feed.** In v2 any failed convertToAssets (`_holdings` -> `_pv` reverts `ExitFailed` on any failed pair read; a zero answer) started the 97 % fallback, so a failing pair or empty bins was priced at 97 % of the last good leg for up to 24 h and the depeg x w bound did not apply (worst case the whole debt, 99.5 USDC / 200 by the borrow cap). **v3 falls back ONLY on the vault's own `FeedInvalid(uint8)`**: a failing pair read, a zero NAV, an empty revert (out of gas), a panic or any other custom error now price 0 at once (T D5, D6, S3, S4, S4b). The "any vault failure" exposure no longer exists; the depeg x w bound of item 4 is the whole fallback exposure. Precisely which bad-feed states still start the fallback (all are `FeedInvalid`, so all are covered by the bound of item 4 and the haircut): the USDT feed older than 93,600 s (26 h) OR, at once, a USDT answer <= 0, a reverting or short-data feed, an incomplete round (`answeredInRound < roundId`), a future-dated `updatedAt` (T D7, S2); the same states of the USDC feed only reach the fallback if the base oracle still prices USDC, which under the pilot config it does not (T D8). An out-of-band answer (a feed that says USDT = 0.6) does NOT revert the vault: it is the live price, unlimited (earlier doc f1).
6. **Interaction checks (all T).** Fallback borrow power = 97 % of live at the same state (49.999 vs 48.499 USDC for 100 shares, T E2); it can exceed the CURRENT live only if the NAV fell more than 3 % after the last poke and nobody poked (T E1b: +2.1 % at a 10 % USDT mark; a USDT feed that merely goes stale cannot do it because the last feed update is older than the 24 h window); the haircut is applied in every reachable state (fuzz E3) and the fallback never exceeds 97 % of the highest vault leg any poke ever saw; a donation during the outage changes nothing (E4); shares donated to the pToken change nothing (E5); forced fallback through the caller's gas never happens (F1/F2: 4k-400k gas in steps of 250 and 90k-106k in steps of 5, every call reverts or returns the live price).
7. **Constants: keep all four** (haircut 3 %, window 24 h, up-cap 2 %, up-interval 1 h). Alternatives and numbers in 9.8. **Residual risks of v3** (9.7): (1) the poke walk during a held glitch, stored x 1.02^n (x1.673 at 26 pokes, x1.707 at 27, never the 2.06 the CF-50 % break-even needs before the vault reverts); (2) a BAD USDT answer (<= 0, reverting feed, incomplete round, future-dated) starts the fallback at once instead of after 26 h, with the pre-outage stored value, so a NAV fall since the last poke is not yet in it (fallback up to +2.1 % above live in the printed case); (3) cadence dependency: the keeper must poke at least hourly (10-15 min is better) or the window is partly spent when the outage starts, and with no poke in the 24 h before it, the price is 0. Fallback validity in the worst case with an hourly keeper: 82,799 s (23 h 0 m) from the vault's first `FeedInvalid`, price 0 at the latest 49 h after the feed's last update (9.1).



## 0. Verdict

1. **Donating USDC or USDt to the vault, or tokens to the pair, cannot make borrowing profitable.** A donation is real, redeemable value: `totalAssets()` counts only token balances the vault owns plus its share of bin reserves, and `redeemInKind` pays exactly that back to share holders, pro rata. The donor gets back at most his own fraction f of what he gave; everyone else keeps the rest. At collateral factor 50 % an attacker needs the oracle to overstate his collateral by more than **2x** (more than 100 %) before borrowing beats paying for it. Nothing an attacker controls overstates it by more than a few basis points (section 3). 256-run fuzz over deposit size, donation size and the fraction c of his shares he posts as collateral (the rest he keeps and exits in kind), plus a 12-point grid including a flash-funded donation of the margin stack's 100 USDC (fee 0): the attacker loses money in every case and the lender's loss is 0 (up to 2 wei of dust) (T). With P = (D + f d)(1 - c (1 - CF)) - D - d, the best case for him is c = 0 (borrow nothing): P = -(1 - f) d, never positive.
2. **Swaps through the pool barely move the share price**, by construction: the vault is a 3-bin, 1 bp-step band, so its NAV marked at the Chainlink ratio can differ from the pool-implied NAV by at most about the band width (1 to 3 bp of the part it converts). Measured in the mock with the feeds at par: a 400,000 USDC swap moved the vault NAV by +0.46 bp (780 wei at USD 17, 9,170 at USD 200). The live pool sits about 10 bp from the oracle ratio (evaluation doc); with the USDT feed set 10 bp off the pool in both signs the same swap moves NAV by up to about **5.5 bp** (offset x converted inventory + band width, T C3). Still basis points against a 2x (10,000 bp) threshold.
3. **The only large overstatement channels are exogenous**, not attacker-controlled: a Chainlink USDT/USD glitch or lag, a USDt depeg the feed lags, a failing Pharaoh hook. With the vault half USDt (today: 49.99 %), a USDT/USD answer of 3.00 doubles `convertToAssets` (T) and exactly reaches the 2x break-even; the guard flags the leg but the share price still follows it (R, T). The USDC/USD leg works the other way round and matters for the limiter: the wrapper's x USDC/USD cancels against pUSDC's own price (the guarded oracle reads the same live feed, no peg clamp, R), so USDC borrow power is CF x `convertToAssets`; a USDC/USD answer of 0.50 raises it by 50 % while the USD share price FALLS 25 % (T F7).
4. **A share-price rate limiter is NOT needed for what was asked** (donation, composition, flash-funded swaps). `[v2]` The v2 wrapper has a ladder on the STORED fallback leg only (+2 % per hour); the LIVE leg is still unlimited, so everything below about the live leg stands, and the verdict below is unchanged. It is a cheap insurance against the feed-glitch / unknown-unknown case only. A tested, optional, one-slot-per-vault design (it limits ASSETS PER SHARE, not the USD price, so it also binds on the USDC-leg glitch) and a patch file are in section 6 (`contracts/test/boosted-manip/patches/oracle-rate-limit.patch`). **Recommendation: do not put it in the 19 Nov wrapper**; ship the current wrapper, keep CF 0 until the fork liquidation test passes, then at most 50 %. If you want it, it has to be in the wrapper you deploy now: adding it later means a second oracle swap (and the operator keepers halt on any `controller.oracle()` change until the new build is on the hosts, BRIEF.md).
5. **Drop side (lenders' real exposure):** a share price that is too HIGH on exit, not too low. Two thresholds on r = realisable / oracle = 1 - w x delta (derived, section 5): liquidators who keep the shares break even only while r >= 0.926 (overstatement <= 7.4 %, because the incentive is 8 %); the lender stays solvent after the feed catches up while r >= 0.54 (CF 50 % x 1.08), i.e. w x delta up to 46 %. Between the two, liquidations are unprofitable and wait for the feed. Two operational behaviours to accept or monitor: `[v2]` ~~price 0 on a stale feed freezes borrow, redeem and liquidation for every account in the share market~~ (v1 behaviour; in v2 this happens only when the stored leg is older than 24 h, the USDC price is missing or the vault is broken, sections 00 and 9); a reverting Pharaoh hook blocks exit while NAV still prices the shares (T).

## 4. The vectors in the order asked

**(a) Donation to the vault.** Modelled with the real vault, the real flash vault and the real oracle wrapper (`FlashDonator`: flash loan 100, donate, supply shares, borrow, repay, all in one callback). The oracle price rises with the donation (A1: collateral valued 274.5 against 183 paid in), the lender hands out 137.25, and the shares are worth 274.5 on exit because the donation sits in the vault. The attacker ends -145.75. Fuzz `testFuzz_A4` (256 runs, D up to the room, d up to 1e9 USDC, c from 0 to 100 %) asserts P <= 0 and lender loss <= 2 wei every time, and that the oracle value never exceeds the in-kind exit value by more than 6 wei.
**(b) Direct transfer to the pair.** NAV reads bin reserves (storage), so it does not move (B1). The tokens are consumed by the next swap or mint (B2, B3). No path credits the donor.
**(c) Swaps.** Section 3. The flash vault only provides 100 USDC; a 400,000 swap needed 400,000 of the attacker's capital and lost USD 70 to move NAV by under USD 0.01.
**(d) Sandwiches.** NAV is oracle-marked and continuous, so entering before a re-centre buys the cost of the re-centre pro rata (D1). Pushing the pool to force a re-centre and trading behind it loses to the pool's depth (D2). Harvest does nothing to NAV (D3). A real, small LP-side loss exists: forced re-centres are bounded at 50 bp each and 48 a day (a theoretical ceiling of 21 to 24 % a day if every one could be forced at the floor, which would cost the attacker far more in pool slippage than the vault loses; E). That is a share price DROP, handled in section 5.
**(e) Mechanics.** No 24 h rule exists. Deposit cap: entries only; TA can exceed it by donation (E3, A5). Min supply: deposit side only; the dead seed is the real floor (E1). A first-depositor style attack on the vault is closed by the dead seed (rounding loss per entry <= one share-wei, i.e. 1e-6 x the share price in USD; at a USD 1M donation that is about USD 0.06; E).
**(f) Drops.** Section 5.

## 5. Share-price drops and NAV that overstates the exit (separate from the limiter)

Lenders only lose if collateral seized and exited is worth less than the debt. Debt <= CF x oracle value; liquidators receive 1.08 x repaid debt in collateral at the ORACLE price and keep the shares. With r = realisable / oracle = 1 - w x delta (derived arithmetic, T F1 prints the table; r = 0 is the hook-down case):

## 6. Share-price rate limiter: verdict and optional design

`[v2]` This section was written for the v1 wrapper (`PharaohVaultShareOracle`, base `e08e5363`); the patch does not apply to `PharaohDlmmShareOracle`. The v2 wrapper already contains a limiter for the stored fallback leg (the poke ladder, section 9.2). A limiter on the LIVE leg would be a subclass overriding `_guardPrice` (the extension point exists) and means a second wrapper deployment and oracle swap; verdict unchanged: not needed for donation/composition/swaps, optional insurance against a feed glitch on the live path.

**Needed for donation / composition manipulation: no** (sections 3 and 4). **Useful for:** an unbacked jump in assets per share: a Chainlink glitch on EITHER feed (USDT/USD high, or USDC/USD low: the latter lowers the USD price while raising USDC borrow power, so a limiter on the USD price would never bind), a bug or upgrade in the pair, hook or vault. Not useful for drops (they pass through at once by design), nor for a USDC/USD glitch UPWARD (it cancels against pUSDC's own price; borrowing other assets is paused in the pilot).

**Design** (patch `contracts/test/boosted-manip/patches/oracle-rate-limit.patch`, base = `contracts/contracts/PharaohVaultShareOracle.sol` at `e08e5363`, identical in `.wt-bp-core` today; `git apply --check` passes; the tested copy is `tests/rl/PharaohVaultShareOracle.sol`, the same text with relative imports):

- What is limited: `convertToAssets(1 whole share)` (asset units) inside `_price`, before the USD conversion. One storage slot per vault: `Checkpoint{uint128 assetsPerShare; uint64 updatedAt}`. Constants `MAX_RISE_BPS_PER_HOUR = 100`, `MAX_ELAPSED = 24 hours`.
- View: `cap = checkpoint x (1 + 1 % x min(elapsed, 24 h) / 1 h)`; the controller reads `min(spot, cap)`. No checkpoint -> spot. A fall is returned immediately.
- **Elapsed time is clamped to 24 h**, so a checkpoint nobody pokes keeps binding: it allows +24 % and no more (stale-low only while a legitimate rise is waiting, which is ppm a day). Without the clamp a stale checkpoint would dissolve.
- `registerVault` stores the current value. `removeVault` deletes it. `poke(vaultShare)` is permissionless: stores `min(spot, cap)` at `now`, **only if the full price read is valid** (both vault feeds fresh and the wrapper's USDC feed fresh), else it does nothing. Poking cannot accelerate the rise: each poke re-bases, so an hourly poke compounds 1 % an hour (doubling takes about 70 h, 1.01^24 = +26.97 % per day, T RL2); 60 pokes in one hour give <= +1.01 % (T RL4).
- Who pays: any keeper, about 50k gas hourly (a cent on Avalanche, E). The operator/treasury keepers already running are the natural caller. Nothing in the lending path depends on it.
- Failure behaviour: feed or vault read failure -> price 0 exactly as today (T RL6), poke is a no-op; no poke for a long time -> capped at +24 % over the last checkpoint (stale-low, T RL5), never 0, never a revert.
- Parameter 100 bp/h: legitimate rises are tiny (fee income and re-centre cost move the price by ppm, T RL7; a feed returning to par is <= 0.5 % x w; an operator top-up of USD 1 on 17 is +5.9 % and is released in 6 hourly pokes). A 3x USDT/USD glitch that lasts 3 hours moves the price <= 3.04 % (T RL8); a 0.50 USDC/USD glitch leaves USDC borrow power unchanged in its block (T RL9); doubling needs about 70 hours of sustained glitch with hourly pokes, longer than a 24 h-heartbeat feed fault plausibly runs. 50 bp/h would also pass the legitimate cases; 100 is a round, conservative choice (E).
- Tests: `tests/RateLimit.t.sol` (9): checkpoint at registration, donation spike held then released at 1 %/h, drops immediate, poke cannot accelerate, stale checkpoint keeps binding, failed feed -> 0 and poke no-op, income plus a real re-centre unchanged, 3x USDT glitch held, 0.50 USDC glitch held.
- Costs: one more storage slot per vault, one public function, a keeper dependency for release of legitimate rises, a changed `getShareUsdPrice` meaning (added `getRawShareUsdPrice` for diagnostics). Not audited.

Alternatives considered and rejected: a TWAP (needs on-chain history or the pair oracle, which has size 0), `min(convertToAssets, previewRedeem)` (same function), and a borrow-power haircut (equals a lower CF: already the lever).

### 9.1 Timeline of a USDT/USD outage (fixture trap, T A1, A2)

| Time after the feed's last update u | What the controller is served |
|---|---|
| 0 .. 93,600 s | the vault still answers (maxFeedAge is inclusive): LIVE price at the stale mark, **no haircut** (pre-existing, same as v1 and any version) |
| 93,601 s | the vault reverts `FeedInvalid`: fallback = floor(stored x 0.97) x live USDC price, provided the last poke is not older than 86,400 s (inclusive) |
| last poke + 86,400 s | last second of the fallback; one second later price 0 (PRICE_ERROR) |

The stored leg expires 86,400 s after the LAST POKE but the vault reverts only 93,601 s after the feed's last update, so a wrapper that nobody pokes during the final day before the outage is already expired when the vault first reverts (price 0, the v1 behaviour: T A2). With an hourly keeper the fallback has 82,799 s (23 h) left at the outage start (T A1). Heartbeat facts (BRIEF-v2): 86,400 s, worst observed age 86,495 s over 5.3 years, so the vault has never reverted on a feed in history: the fallback covers an event that has not been observed.

**How long is the fallback valid after the vault's first `FeedInvalid` (T A4)?** Let u be the feed's last update. The vault answers until u + 93,600 (inclusive), so the last poke that can store a value is at most u + 93,600, and the fallback is valid until lastPoke + 86,400 (inclusive). Seconds of fallback left at the first revert (u + 93,601) = 86,400 - (93,601 - lastPoke), and price 0 from lastPoke + 86,401. By keeper cadence c (every poke accepted, the NAV flat; printed by A4):

| cadence | last poke after u | fallback valid from the first revert | price 0 from (after u) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 min | u + 93,600 | 86,399 s (24.0 h) | u + 180,001 s (50.0 h) |
| 1 h, best phase | u + 93,600 | 86,399 s | u + 180,001 s |
| **1 h, worst phase** | u + 90,000 (A1) | **82,799 s (23 h 0 m)** | u + 176,401 s (49.0 h) |
| 6 h | u + 86,400 | 79,199 s (22.0 h) | u + 172,801 s |
| 11.1 h | u + 80,000 | 72,799 s (20.2 h) | u + 166,401 s |

So with an hourly keeper the fallback lasts between 23.0 h and 24.0 h from the first `FeedInvalid` (worst phase: last accepted poke 3,600 s before the first revert, 82,799 s as in A1), after 26 h of the un-haircut stale live mark: price 0 at 49-50 h after the feed's last update. A poke refused by the 1 h gate (live above stored inside the gate) does not move the clock, so a keeper that polls every few minutes should still expect its last ACCEPTED poke up to 1 h before. For the immediate triggers of 9.4 (bad USDT answer) the window is measured from the last accepted poke, not from a 93,601 s delay: 86,400 - c.

Upward poisoning, quantified: the stored leg can exceed the TRUE value only through an exogenous glitch (donations are backed), by at most 1.02^n after n hourly pokes during it, i.e. x1.61 in 24 h and x2.04 in 36 h; the cap of the glitch itself is the time until the feed goes stale (26 h: x1.673 stored, x1.707 with a 27th poke, x1.62-x1.66 fallback, below the x2.06 that the CF-50 % break-even needs). Since the live price during a glitch is unlimited, the poke ladder never makes a glitch worse than it already is.

**Downward poisoning and the ratchet trap (T C1, C2).** Anyone can store a lower vault answer at once. After a one-block -25 % glitch the stored leg needs 15 hourly pokes to climb back. Hours to recover, by dip and up-step: 

| dip | 1 % / h | 2 % / h | 5 % / h |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 % | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 3 % | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| 5 % | 6 | 3 | 2 |
| 10 % | 11 | 6 | 3 |
| 25 % | 29 | 15 | 6 |
| 50 % | 70 | 36 | 15 |
| 75 % | 140 | 71 | 29 |

Consequence: a low stored leg is used only when the vault cannot answer. (i) For a USDT feed that goes stale, the glitch's correcting update is the feed's last update, so the outage starts 93,601 s later and 26 hourly pokes have run: the trap survives into the outage only if 1.02^26 = 1.673 x the low value is still below live, i.e. a dip above **40.2 %** (T C2; 41.4 % if a 27th poke fits). (ii) With a -50 % dip the stored leg is 83.7 % of true at the outage start; a borrower who opened at LTV 45 % of the live value after the recovery is liquidatable on the 0.97 x 0.837 price; the liquidator's bonus at TRUE prices is 33 % (13,306 bp, vs 8 % normal), paid by the borrower; the collateral left covers the remaining debt more than twice: **no lender loss** (T C2). (iii) Anything that was already liquidatable at the glitch's own live price was exposed by the live path (pre-existing); the trap adds only accounts opened after the recovery. (iv) With the non-feed triggers of 9.4 the overlap is possible at any dip size; the loss is the same kind (borrower to liquidator).

**MEV: can anyone profit by choosing WHEN to poke?** The poke writes a value that is at most the vault's own answer at that instant. Upward timing: the value is backed (donation) or exogenous; it only raises a price that is read in a fallback, the best case for an attacker being +2 % (1.9 USDC of extra borrow power on a 96.5 USDC maximum) for a real donation of 2 % of the NAV. Downward timing: needs a transient low; no attacker-controlled action lowers the leg (T C3: sandwiches and pushes are >= 0 within rounding; the forced re-centre sandwich costs the vault <= 0.012 %, earlier doc d2), so the only lows are exogenous; locking one in is harmless unless an outage starts inside the climb (needs the 40 % dip above, or a non-feed outage) and then costs borrowers, not lenders. Front-running the keeper's poke changes nothing: pokes are idempotent per state. Gas: about 33k (steady state) to 110k (first, cold) per accepted poke (E, from the traces).

### 9.3 The fallback overvaluation window (key economic question)

**What can lower the true NAV while the vault is unavailable?** Per the vault source (R): NAV = idle USDC + idle USDt + the vault's pro-rata share of three 1-bp bins, the USDt marked with the two feeds. In an outage the feed mark is frozen, so the value that matters is the market value of the USDt the vault holds. (a) A USDt depeg delta: the true NAV changes by w x delta, w = USDt fraction; after a real depeg arbitrageurs sell USDt into the pool, the pool price leaves the band within a few bp and the vault is left holding USDt only (T D1: w 49.99 % before the pool moves, 99.99 % after, with the real vault and the mock pool). So the worst case is w = 1 whatever the composition was (idle USDC deposits that the keeper has not yet re-centred dilute w: seen while writing C2, where a 100 USDC deposit made a total USDT glitch cost the NAV 7 % instead of 50 %). (b) Re-centre losses: not possible in the outage, the keeper's `rebalance` calls `_requireEntryOk()` and `_prices()`, which revert on a bad feed (R, vault lines 418-446); `redeemInKind` stays open (ungated); fees and income are ppm. (c) USDC depeg: lifts the USDC-unit NAV and is priced by the live USDC feed (the fallback uses the live base price); a USDC feed outage is not a fallback case (9.5). (d) Non-feed vault failures: 9.4.

### 9.4 Which vault failures start the fallback: v2 (any) versus v3 (`FeedInvalid` only) (T D5-D7, S1-S4b)

Finding on v2 (resolved): BRIEF-v2 stated the vault reverts only on a bad feed; the source says otherwise (R): `_holdings` reads the pair through `_pv`, which `revert ExitFailed()` on any failed or short pair read, and v2 also treated a zero `convertToAssets` as "cannot answer". With fresh feeds, a pair whose `getBin` reverted, or a pair reporting empty bins (TA 2 wei, `convertToAssets(1 share)` = 0), was priced by v2 at 97 % of the last good leg for up to 24 h; the true NAV there is unrelated to w x delta and the bound was the whole debt (0.5 x posted <= 99.5 USDC at the current size, 200 by the borrow cap).

**v3 (`cac947c9`)**: the wrapper reads the vault's revert data: only `FeedInvalid(uint8)` (the vault's error for a bad or stale Chainlink feed) is an outage. Everything else is a broken vault, price 0 at once, no fallback, `poke()` returns false: a failing pair read (`ExitFailed`), an empty revert (what an out-of-gas inside the vault looks like), a Panic, any other custom error, a zero answer, a short return, an oversized answer. Tests: D5 (failing pair, fresh feeds: price 0, no fallback), D6 (NAV 2 wei: 0; a tiny non-zero answer is the live price), S3, S4, S4b (empty revert, Panic, `ExitFailed` give 0; `FeedInvalid(1)` and `FeedInvalid(2)` give the fallback). A partial collapse (NAV 1 % of before) is still the live price, not affected. Two consequences to know: (a) it depends on the vault's error name: an upgrade that renames the error fails closed (price 0, the v1 behaviour); (b) when the vault call itself runs out of gas the wrapper may return 0 instead of reverting (about 310 of 1,585 swept gas values, 4k-400k): fail closed, it blinds only the caller's own transaction.

**Bad-feed states that still start the fallback in v3** (the vault's `_feeds()` marks the feed not-ok, `_prices()` reverts `FeedInvalid`): the USDT feed (a) older than 93,600 s, (b) answer <= 0, (c) latestRoundData reverting or returning < 160 bytes, (d) incomplete round (`answeredInRound < roundId`), (e) `updatedAt` in the future or 0 (T D7, S2). Cases (b)-(e) start the fallback at once, without the 26 h of stale live mark, with whatever the last poke stored. All of them are Chainlink-side; no caller can set them. NOT a fallback case: an out-of-band answer (0.6, 3.0): the vault answers, the live price follows it (the unlimited live path, T B4).

### 9.5 USDC side (T D8)

The base oracle's USDC maxAge 90,000 s is below the vault's 93,600 s, and the vault's and the base's USDC feed is the same contract (`0xF096...23B9`, R from the vault rehearsal report). So a stale USDC feed is already priced 0 by the base oracle before the vault reverts: price 0, never the fallback (D8 a, b). An incomplete round or a future-dated update is rejected by both sides (c, d). The base additionally rejects `roundId == 0`, which the vault accepts: price 0 as well. **The only USDC-side path to the fallback is an owner configuration**: the source oracle's USDC maxAge raised above 93,600 s (D8 e). Do not widen it.

### 9.6 Interaction checks (T)

- **Borrow power, fallback vs live** (E1, E1b, E2): at the same state the fallback is 97 % of live (100 shares at CF 50 %: 49.99935 vs 48.49935 USDC, 9,699 bp after floors). It is above the CURRENT live only when the NAV fell more than 3 % after the last poke and nobody poked (printed: a 10 % USDT mark, NAV -5 %, fallback / live = 10,210 bp); one permissionless poke of the drop removes it, so the keeper cadence matters (10-15 min). A stale USDT feed cannot produce this case (its last update is older than the 24 h window); a bad/reverting USDT answer can (in v2 a pair failure could too; v3 prices that 0). Fuzz E1 (256 runs): fallback <= live iff live >= 0.97 x stored.
- **Haircut cannot be bypassed** (E3, fuzz 256 runs of random donations/glitches/time/pokes followed by 26 hourly pokes and the outage): the price is exactly floor(stored x 0.97) x live USDC price, `getShareUsdPrice` agrees, and it never exceeds 97 % of the highest vault leg any accepted poke saw. No function returns the stored leg undiscounted; `shareState()` shows it raw for monitoring only.
- **Donation during the outage** (E4): 500 USDC and 500 USDt donated, price unchanged, `poke()` false, stored unchanged, live 0. After the feed returns the live price is the backed value and the stored leg can then only step +2 % per hour.
- **Seed/donation inflation of the market is unchanged** (E5): the wrapper reads only the `market` address and the vault; shares donated to the pToken address (16 and 1 shares) move neither the live nor the fallback price. The seed-first mint and the flash-loan pause live in `PharaohBoostedDelegate`, which differs from the pre-v2 core (`2050f9a8`) by nothing (`git diff 2050f9a8 2ec28007` on the delegate, PToken and PErc20 is empty); the core suite checks them (section 8).
- **Forced fallback through the caller's gas** (F1, F1b, F2, F3): `getUnderlyingPrice{gas: g}` for g = 4,000..400,000 (step 250) and 90,000..106,000 (step 5), with the stored leg above live (the discriminating case: fallback 1.009 vs live 1.000) and below it, access lists cooled: every call reverts (g below about 100k), returns exactly the live price or, in v3, returns 0 where the vault read ran out of gas (about a fifth of the swept values; never the fallback price): 0 other results in 3,201 fine-sweep calls (2,012 reverts, 1,189 live) and in 1,585 coarse calls per scenario (385 reverts, 1,200 live). `poke{gas: g}` either stores nothing or stores exactly the bounded live leg. Reason (E): when the vault call runs out of gas inside the 63/64 rule, 1/64 of the caller's gas (about 1.5k) cannot finish the base-oracle read that follows, so the whole call reverts. Not a proof for every EVM version or access pattern.

### 9.7 Operations that follow

1. A keeper that pokes at least hourly, ideally every 10-15 minutes (downward moves are accepted at once; upward moves are throttled to hourly anyway): needed so that (a) the window is fresh when an outage starts (9.1), (b) the stored leg is not stale-high after a real fall (9.6). `tools/bp-dlmm-poke.sh` exists on the core branch.
2. Alerts: `shareState().usingFallback` true (page at once; under v3 it means a Chainlink-side `FeedInvalid`); `getShareUsdPrice()` = 0 while both vault feeds are fresh = a non-feed vault failure (v3 prices it 0, 9.4); `fallbackSecondsLeft` below 4 h; live vs stored differing by more than 2 %.
3. Raising the share market's CF past 0 only after the fork liquidation test, at most 50 % (unchanged); with v2/v3, new borrowers should be told the effective limit during an outage is 48.5 % of stored.

### 9.8 Constants: keep or change (nothing was changed in the wrapper)

The discriminator: the collateral factor, not the haircut, keeps lenders whole (bad debt starts at 47.6 % of NAV loss at 3 %, at 51.4 % at a 10 % haircut). Arithmetic of the alternatives (T G1, CF 50 %, incentive 108 %):

| Haircut | LTV liquidatable at outage start | liquidation at fallback prices pays up to w x delta | pop B bad debt starts above w x delta | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 % (now) | 48.5 % | 10.2 % | 47.6 % | - |
| 5 % | 47.5 % | 12.0 % | 48.7 % | 2 pp less borrow power in an outage |
| 10 % | 45.0 % | 16.7 % | 51.4 % | accounts above 45 % are liquidated the moment any outage begins |

| Up-step | hours to climb back after -25 % | hours until 0.97 x stored >= 2 x true under a sustained glitch | stored after 24 h of poisoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 % per h | 30 | 73 | x1.269 |
| 2 % per h (now) | 15 | 37 | x1.607 |
| 5 % per h | 6 | 15 | x3.223 |

Window (arithmetic, E): the window does not change the per-depeg bound, it changes for how long a depeg can develop and persist unpriced. With an hourly keeper the fallback lasts 11 h / 23 h / 47 h for a 12 / 24 / 48 h window. The vault has never reverted on a feed in 5.3 years (worst age 86,495 s), so no window can be calibrated on data; 12 h turns most outages into price 0 (freeze, including no liquidations) and 48 h keeps pricing a stale mark for two days; the lender buffer (46-47.6 %) dwarfs what a day of stable-coin drift can do, while a freeze blocks liquidation of every account in the market.

**Recommendation: keep all four** (3 %, 24 h, 2 %, 1 h). The one change worth making, the `FeedInvalid`-only fallback, is in v3. The haircut moves lender safety by under 4 pp of NAV loss even at 10 %; 5 % is defensible if you want margin over a USDT dip of about 5 % (USDT traded near 0.95 briefly in May 2022: from memory, not verified here) but costs borrowers the top 2 pp of their limit during outages. A 2 % step is the middle: 1 % leaves a 25 % dip unrepaired for 30 h; 5 % lets a glitch double the stored leg in 15 h. The 1 h interval is irrelevant to safety (the step per hour is what counts); keep it. Not recommended as wrapper changes: any of the above; the remaining items are operational (9.7).
